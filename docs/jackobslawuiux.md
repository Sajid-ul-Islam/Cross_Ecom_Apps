# The Jakob's Law Blueprint for UX Navigation

## What is Jakob's Law?
Jakob's Law is named after a UX researcher who holds over 79 patents [1]. The core principle is that because people spend almost all of their time in other apps, they expect your app to work the exact same way [1]. When designing an app, you should not reinvent the navigation, but instead use the basic layouts already established by giants like Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube [1].

## Key Rules for App Navigation
To ensure you meet user expectations, follow these basic navigation rules:
1. **Limit your tabs:** Have a maximum of five tabs [1].
2. **Home placement:** The "Home" tab should be located on the far left [1].
3. **Profile placement:** The "Profile" tab should be located on the far right [1].
4. **Creation button:** If your app allows users to create something, place that action directly in the middle [1].
5. **Standard gestures:** Swiping from the left edge should take the user back, and pulling down should refresh the content [1].

## Common Pitfalls and Lessons Learned
- **Reserve Bottom Navigation for Core Actions:** Avoid giving "Settings" its own tab at the bottom, as bottom navigation must be reserved for the app's core actions [1]. Settings should be placed either in the top right or within the profile section [1].
- **Avoid Unexpected Placements:** Placing features in unexpected areas forces users to learn your app from scratch, which directly hurts user retention [1].
- **The Snapchat Redesign Failure:** Snapchat learned the dangers of breaking these rules the hard way in 2018; after redesigning familiar parts of the app, more than 1.2 million people signed a petition asking the company to reverse the redesign [1]. 

*Note: You can examine the apps you spend the most time on to find and use these familiar designs within your own app [1].*
